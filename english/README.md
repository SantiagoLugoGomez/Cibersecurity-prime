# English for cybersecurity (C1 to C2)

Technical English is not optional in this field. The best documentation, the most advisories, the certification exams, the bug bounty platforms and most high-paying remote roles are in English. Your general English is already strong (B2-C1), so this track is narrow on purpose: it targets the professional and technical register you need to read advisories fluently, write reports that read as native, and speak about attacks and defenses under pressure.

C2 in this context does not mean sounding like a dictionary. It means precision (the exact term, used correctly), fluency (no hunting for words mid-sentence in an interview), and register control (formal in a report, plain in a standup, careful in a disclosure email).

## How to use this track

- Spend one weekly block here (see [weekly-plan.md](../weekly-plan.md)). It is small and steady, not a separate course.
- Tie it to what you are already doing. The text you read for a lab is also your reading practice; the writeup you owe is also your writing practice.
- Keep a personal vocabulary file. Every time you meet a term you could not have produced yourself, add it with a real example sentence from where you found it.
- Measure twice a year. Take a free [EF SET](https://www.efset.org/ef-set-50/) reading and listening test now for a baseline, and again in six months. It is free and maps to the CEFR scale. For productive skills, judge yourself by whether a native reviewer changes your report's wording and whether you can answer an interview question without stalling.

## What C2 looks like here

- [ ] You read a CISA advisory or a CVE description once and understand it fully, including the hedging and the conditionals.
- [ ] You write a finding that a native-speaking reviewer corrects for content, not for grammar or word choice.
- [ ] You explain a vulnerability out loud for two minutes without searching for a word.
- [ ] You use the exact term (mitigate vs remediate, vulnerability vs exploit vs threat) without thinking about it.
- [ ] You can shift register: formal in a report, direct in a standup, careful and neutral in a disclosure email.
