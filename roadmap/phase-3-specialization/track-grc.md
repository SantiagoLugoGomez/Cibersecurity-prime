# Track deep dive: Governance, risk and compliance (12 weeks)

For the student who chose GRC. The work is less hands-on technically, but people who understand the technology behind the controls are far more effective and valued, and your technical phases are exactly that advantage. This curriculum, with its Colombian focus, takes you from the Phase 1 frameworks baseline to producing the real artifacts a GRC analyst or IT auditor delivers.

**Prerequisites:** Phase 1 complete, especially the frameworks and governance module.
**Main resources:** [NIST CSF 2.0](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework), [CIS Controls v8.1](https://www.cisecurity.org/controls/v8-1), [ISO/IEC 27001](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001) (overview is free), Colombian regulations (see [legal-and-ethics.md](../../legal-and-ethics.md)), [SANS policy templates](https://www.sans.org/information-security-policy/) (free).
**Certification target:** ISO/IEC 27001 Lead Implementer or Lead Auditor; later CISA or CISM (see [Phase 4](../phase-4-professional/README.md#stage-3-track-certification-one-in-your-primary-track)).

## Weeks 1-2: Standards and frameworks in depth

**Read/study:** ISO/IEC 27001 structure (the ISMS and the clauses) and 27002:2022 (93 controls in four themes: organizational, people, physical, technological); how CSF, CIS and ISO relate.
**Practice:** map ten CIS IG1 safeguards to both CSF functions and ISO 27002 controls in a single crosswalk table.
**Deep practice:** build a complete framework crosswalk for your fictional company: CSF function to ISO 27002 theme to CIS control, so you can speak any of the three languages.
**English:** write a one-page explainer of how the three frameworks fit together.
**Deliverable:** a framework crosswalk table.

## Weeks 3-4: Risk management

**Read/study:** risk assessment methodologies (qualitative and quantitative); [NIST SP 800-30](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/30/r1/final) risk assessment; risk appetite and treatment.
**Practice:** build a full risk register for the fictional company: 15+ risks with owner, likelihood, impact, inherent and residual ratings, and treatment decisions.
**Deep practice:** run a structured risk assessment for one business process end to end, from asset identification through threat and vulnerability analysis to a prioritized risk treatment plan.
**English:** write the risk assessment report.
**Deliverable:** a risk register and one full process risk assessment.

## Weeks 5-6: Policies and an ISMS

**Read/study:** the policy/standard/procedure hierarchy; what an ISMS requires (scope, statement of applicability, objectives, continual improvement); [SANS policy templates](https://www.sans.org/information-security-policy/).
**Practice:** write three policies (information security, acceptable use, access control) aligned to ISO 27001 and referencing Colombian law.
**Deep practice:** draft a mini ISMS for the fictional company: scope statement, a short statement of applicability selecting relevant 27002 controls, and the top-level policy. This is the core document set of a certified organization.
**English:** write the information security policy and statement of applicability in English.
**Deliverable:** three policies and a mini ISMS document set.

## Weeks 7-8: Auditing and control testing

**Read/study:** how audits work (evidence, sampling, findings, nonconformities); the auditor's mindset; control design vs operating effectiveness.
**Practice:** design test procedures for ten controls (how would you prove each one actually works?); gather evidence for a few in your own lab.
**Deep practice:** audit your own lab against CIS IG1: for each safeguard, record whether it is implemented, the evidence, and a finding where it is not. Produce an audit report with findings and recommendations.
**English:** write the audit report with clear, evidence-based findings.
**Deliverable:** a CIS IG1 audit of your lab with a findings report.

## Weeks 9-10: Colombian and sector compliance

**Read/study:** [Ley 1581 de 2012](https://www.suin-juriscol.gov.co/viewDocument.asp?ruta=Leyes/1684507) and its regulation in depth; MinTIC's MSPI (updated 2025 to align with ISO 27001:2022) for the public sector; the Superintendencia Financiera's cybersecurity circular (CE 007 de 2018) for finance.
**Practice:** build a Ley 1581 compliance checklist; map the firm's obligations and gaps.
**Deep practice:** produce a data-protection compliance assessment for the fictional company under Ley 1581: what personal data it handles, the legal bases, the gaps, and a remediation plan. If the firm were financial or public sector, add the relevant sector obligations.
**English:** write the executive summary; the body can be bilingual, matching real Colombian practice.
**Deliverable:** a Ley 1581 compliance assessment with a remediation plan.

## Weeks 11-12: Capstone GRC program

**Deep practice:** assemble everything into a coherent security program for the fictional company: the framework crosswalk, the risk register, the ISMS core documents, the policies, the audit findings and the compliance assessment, with a one-page board-level summary and a prioritized roadmap. This is exactly what a GRC lead or vCISO presents, and it is your portfolio centerpiece.
**English:** the board summary and roadmap in polished English.
**Deliverable:** a complete GRC program package for the fictional company.

## You are ready for the certification when...

- [ ] You can speak CSF, ISO 27001/27002 and CIS and translate between them.
- [ ] You can run a risk assessment and produce a defensible risk register.
- [ ] You can draft an ISMS core document set and real policies.
- [ ] You can audit controls against a standard and write evidence-based findings.
- [ ] You can assess Ley 1581 compliance and build a remediation plan.
- [ ] You have a full GRC program package in your portfolio.

**Note:** keep your technical edge. A GRC analyst who can also read a config, a log or a scan result is worth far more than one who only reads spreadsheets. Revisit a technical track's basics periodically.

**Free-only path:** the frameworks (CSF, CIS), NIST publications, ISO overviews, SANS policy templates and the Colombian regulations are all free; only the ISO exams and training cost money, and those are usually employer-funded.
