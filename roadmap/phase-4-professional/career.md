# Getting hired

Certifications and projects open doors, but you still have to find the role, present your work and pass the interview. This page covers the job search for a cybersecurity student and new graduate in Colombia and for remote roles abroad.

## The Colombian and remote market

- **Where the jobs are.** In Colombia, most entry security roles are in banks and fintech (regulated by the Superintendencia Financiera, so they take security seriously), telecoms, consultancies (the big four and local firms), managed security service providers (SOCs), and the public sector. Bogotá and Medellín have the most openings.
- **The SOC is the most common entry point.** Level 1 SOC analyst roles hire new graduates and even advanced students, often on shifts. They are a strong first job even if you want to go offensive later.
- **Remote roles abroad.** Your B2-C1 English (heading to C2) plus a visible portfolio make remote and nearshore roles for US and European companies realistic. These pay more and value practical certifications and a GitHub portfolio over degrees.
- **Internships (prácticas).** Your degree includes a practice semester. Target it deliberately at a security team; start applying two semesters ahead.

## Your portfolio is your CV

By the end of Phase 3 you have real artifacts. Make them findable:

- **This repository**, showing the roadmap you followed and your progress.
- **Writeups and reports** (sanitized): machine writeups, the web assessment, the incident reports, the AD attack-and-detect, your track capstone.
- **Tools** you built (the Python and Bash tools from Phase 0, the cloud checker, the detection set).
- **A short portfolio README** linking the three or four best pieces for your chosen track.

Pin the strongest repositories on your GitHub profile. A hiring manager who sees a clean report and a working tool trusts you more than one who sees only a certificate logo.

## CV and LinkedIn

- **One-page CV in English** (and a Spanish version for local roles). Lead with a two-line summary, then skills, then projects with links, then education and certifications. Quantify where you can ("compromised a 3-host AD lab from external foothold to Domain Admin; wrote the engagement report").
- **LinkedIn in English**, with the same summary, your projects in the Featured section, and the certifications. Follow and engage with the Colombian and LatAm security community (see [community.md](../../community.md)).
- **Link the repo** from both. The portfolio is the differentiator.
- Keep both honest. Never claim a certification you do not hold or a skill you cannot demonstrate in an interview.

## Interview preparation

Technical interviews for security roles usually have a few parts: a screening call, a technical interview (concepts and sometimes a practical exercise), and a behavioral interview.

- **Rehearse the question bank** in [english/speaking-practice.md](../../english/speaking-practice.md), out loud, in both languages. The general and your-track sections matter most.
- **Be able to walk through one project end to end** from your portfolio: what you did, why, what you found, what you would do differently. Interviewers probe depth here.
- **Practical exercises:** some employers give a take-home (a machine to compromise and report, or logs to investigate). Your Phase 2 and 3 work is direct preparation; treat the take-home like a scoped engagement and deliver a clean report.
- **Behavioral:** prepare short stories about teamwork (your CTF team), learning from failure (a box that beat you), and ethics (how you handle finding something you were not authorized to test, per [legal-and-ethics.md](../../legal-and-ethics.md)).
- **Questions to ask them:** about the team's tooling, how they handle findings, and what a first-year analyst actually does. Good questions signal seniority.

## A realistic timeline

| When | Focus |
|---|---|
| Semesters 2-4 | Build foundations and practice; grow the portfolio; first CTFs |
| Semester 5 | Choose a track; first foundation certification; start networking in the community |
| Semester 6 | First practical certification; apply for a security-focused practice semester |
| Practice semester | Perform in a real team; convert it toward a job offer where possible |
| Final semesters | Track certification; bug bounty or open-source contributions; apply for junior roles and remote positions |

## Staying employable

- Keep learning after you are hired; the field moves fast (recall how much changed between framework versions in this roadmap).
- Keep writing and sharing; a public track record compounds.
- Specialize, but keep a working knowledge of the adjacent tracks. The most valuable people bridge offense and defense, or technology and governance.
- Mentor the students behind you. It builds your reputation and reveals the gaps in your own knowledge.
