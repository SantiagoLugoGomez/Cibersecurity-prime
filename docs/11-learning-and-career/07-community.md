# Community

> [!TIP]
> **In short:** security is a small world, and most opportunities (a job lead, a mentor, a collaboration, a bug bounty invite) come through people, not job boards. Being visible and genuinely helpful in these communities matters as much as any certificate in [Certification strategy](05-certification-strategy.md). Dates, prices and chapter leadership change every year, so treat every specific detail on this page as a starting point to verify on the official source before you plan around it, not as a fixed schedule.

## Colombia

### Conferences and events

| Event | Where | When | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| [DragonJAR Security Conference](https://www.dragonjar.org/) | Medellín | Usually September | The largest Spanish-language offensive security conference in Colombia; talks in Spanish, strong for making local connections |
| [BSides Colombia](https://sessionize.com/bsides-colombia-2026) | Rotates between cities | Usually April | Community conference with talks, workshops, villages and a CTF; explicitly student-friendly |
| [ISACA Latin America and local chapter events](https://engage.isaca.org/) | Bogotá | Varies | Governance, risk and audit focus; connects directly to [Frameworks and standards](../10-governance-risk-and-compliance/02-frameworks-and-standards.md) and the GRC track |

Follow each event's organizers on social media for the next edition's dates, since they move year to year and are rarely fixed to a specific calendar slot.

### Chapters and groups

| Group | Focus | How to join |
|---|---|---|
| [OWASP Bogotá](https://owasp.org/www-chapter-bogota/) | Application and web security, directly reinforcing [Section 5, Application Security](../05-application-security/01-browser-security-model-and-headers.md) | Free meetups; join the chapter's group to get notified of the next one |
| [OWASP Medellín](https://owasp.org/www-chapter-medellin/) | Application security | Free; check the chapter page for current activity |
| ISC2 Colombia Chapter | General security, certifications | Local chapter of ISC2; search for its current page, since chapter sites move over time |
| [WOMCY (LATAM Women in Cybersecurity)](https://womcy.org/) | Women in cybersecurity across Latin America | Non-profit with mentoring and events, open across the region |

### At your university

A public, university-run cybersecurity student group at the Escuela Colombiana de Ingeniería Julio Garavito was not found at the time this was researched. That is an opportunity, not a dead end:

- Ask your Fundamentals of Cybersecurity professor whether a *semillero* (research seed group) already exists, or could be started.
- Start a small CTF team with classmates and register it on [CTFtime](https://ctftime.org/). Three or four committed people is genuinely enough to begin.
- Check the systems engineering department for any research group already touching security, networks or cryptography, even if it does not advertise itself as a security group specifically.

A student-run CTF team or security club is one of the strongest things you can put on a CV, and it is also the easiest, lowest-stakes way to build the habit of practicing alongside others rather than only alone.

## Latin America

| Event | Country | When | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Ekoparty](https://www.ekoparty.org/) | Argentina (Buenos Aires) | Usually October | The longest-running and best-known hacker conference in Latin America; trainings, villages, a CTF |
| [8.8 Computer Security Conference](https://www.8dot8.org/) | Chile (Santiago), with other regional editions | Usually October | Technical offensive security conference |
| [H2HC (Hackers to Hackers Conference)](https://www.h2hc.com.br/) | Brazil (São Paulo) | Usually late in the year | One of the oldest technical conferences in the region |

Latin America also fields a combined regional team, **CyberLATAM**, in the [International Cybersecurity Challenge](https://ecsc.eu/), with Colombia among the participating countries in past editions. A strong CTF performance, individually or with a university team, is one of the more direct ways onto that regional radar.

## Online communities

| Community | What it is | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| [TryHackMe](https://tryhackme.com/) and [Hack The Box](https://www.hackthebox.com/) community chat | Official community spaces | Nudges on rooms and machines without full spoilers, and a way to find teammates |
| [CTFtime](https://ctftime.org/) | CTF calendar and team rankings | Finding online CTFs to join and a team to play with |
| r/netsec, r/AskNetsec, r/cybersecurity | Community discussion spaces | News, career questions, and general discussion |
| Local and regional chat groups | Spanish-language practitioner chat | Many Colombian and Latin American practitioners organize here; ask at conferences for current invites, since these move and churn |
| Practitioner networks (Mastodon and similar) | Researchers sharing findings and advisories directly | A good way to follow the field in something closer to real time than waiting for aggregated news |

**A note on etiquette**, worth internalizing before your first post in any of these: ask specific questions, show what you have already tried, and never ask for or share flags or answers to active, currently gradable challenges. Helping others with *method*, not solutions, is how a reputation in these communities is actually built, and it is the same discipline already set out for your own published work in [Portfolio and writing](06-portfolio-and-writing.md#before-you-publish-an-osint-self-audit).

## People and sources worth following

Keeping up with the field is part of the job, not a separate hobby. Concrete, current sources for ongoing reading and listening practice are listed in the English track, specifically [reading-professional-material.md](../../english/reading-professional-material.md) and [speaking-practice.md](../../english/speaking-practice.md): advisories from national cyber coordination bodies, vendor threat intelligence reports, incident write-up outlets such as The DFIR Report, and podcasts including Darknet Diaries, Risky Business, and the SANS Internet Storm Center's daily StormCast.

## Why this belongs in your weekly routine, not just your calendar

Community engagement compounds the same way the study habits in [the study system](02-the-study-system.md) do: a single conference attended once produces a pleasant memory; a chapter meetup attended regularly, a CTF team played with consistently, and a professional network engaged with weekly produce an actual reputation, real mentors, and real opportunities, over the same timescale the rest of this roadmap already asks you to invest in.

## Practice

1. Join one Colombian or regional group or chapter from the tables above this month, and attend or watch the recording of its next event.
2. Register a team, even a team of one to start, on CTFtime, and play one online CTF in the next quarter, however it goes.
3. Ask your Fundamentals of Cybersecurity professor, this semester, whether a *semillero* or equivalent research group exists or could exist, and report back on what you learn in your notes.
4. Follow one source from the "people and sources worth following" list for a month, and bring one thing you learned from it into a conversation, a writeup, or a flashcard.

These support [Module 4 of the roadmap](../../roadmap/phase-4-professional/README.md#community-and-visibility).

## What to do with this

- [ ] Join at least one community or chapter from this page and actually show up to something, not just subscribe.
- [ ] Register for CTFtime and play at least one event with a team.
- [ ] Ask about starting or joining a student security group at your own university.
- [ ] Follow the etiquette norm (method, not solutions) from your very first post in any community.
- [ ] Build following the field into a weekly habit, not an occasional binge before an interview.
