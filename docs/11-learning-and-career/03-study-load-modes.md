# Study load modes

> [!TIP]
> **In short:** a fixed weekly plan breaks the first time a real semester happens to you: three exams in the same week, a group project deadline, illness. Instead of one plan that either gets followed perfectly or abandoned entirely, this page defines **four modes** (Green, Yellow, Red, Pause) that match the actual load you are carrying right now, with an explicit, honest rule for when to switch between them. The goal is that this roadmap survives a real engineering degree, not that you follow an idealized schedule that assumes nothing else in your life ever happens.

## The four modes

| Mode | When you are in it | Weekly time | What runs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🟢 **Green** | A normal week: no exams this week, courses are at their usual pace | About 7.5 hours | All four sessions: R, P, D, E |
| 🟡 **Yellow** | One heavy week: a single exam or major deadline, but not several at once | About 4 hours | R and daily flashcards always; P or D (pick one, not both), E if time allows |
| 🔴 **Red** | Crisis load: finals week, multiple deadlines colliding, acute illness | About 1.5 to 2 hours | R only, plus daily flashcards; everything else pauses |
| ⏸️ **Pause** | A genuine full stop: a serious illness, a family emergency, a period you have decided in advance to take fully off | 0 hours | Nothing; the schedule explicitly slides, with a written resume plan |

**The rule for switching modes:** decide your mode **at the start of the week**, honestly, based on your actual calendar for that week, not based on how motivated you feel on Monday morning. Switching mid-week because the week turned out harder than expected is normal and fine; switching mid-week from Green to Yellow because you simply did not start is a different problem, and the honest fix is to start, not to redefine the mode.

## Why modes instead of one fixed plan

A plan that assumes every week is Green either gets abandoned during the first hard month of the semester, which trains the habit of all-or-nothing thinking, or it gets followed at the cost of the degree itself, which this roadmap exists to support, not compete with. Defining Yellow and Red explicitly, in advance, means a hard week has a **specific, smaller, still-real plan** waiting for it, instead of an implicit choice between the full plan and nothing. The spacing research in [Learning science](01-learning-science.md#spacing-distribute-practice-over-time) is itself the argument for this: a small amount of consistent contact with the material, even a stripped-down 90 minutes in a brutal week, protects retention far better than a full week of silence followed by a full week of catch-up.

## 🟢 Green: the sustainable default

This is the plan for most weeks of a normal semester, built around the four sessions from [the study system](02-the-study-system.md), spread across real days.

| Day | Session | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | R | 75 min | Read and study the week's material; write flashcards |
| Tuesday | flashcards only | 10 min | Spaced-repetition review |
| Wednesday | P | 90 min | Guided practice: labs, levels, drills |
| Thursday | E | 60 min | English on the week's topic ([english/](../../english/README.md)) |
| Friday | flashcards only | 10 min | Spaced-repetition review |
| Saturday | D | 3 h | Deep practice: a machine, build, tool or investigation, ending in a writeup |
| Sunday | review | 30-45 min | Answer check-yourself questions, commit the deliverable, update [progress.md](../../roadmap/progress.md), plan next week |

This schedule overlaps deliberately with your coursework (see [Your degree and security](04-your-degree-and-security.md)) so the two reinforce each other instead of competing for separate hours.

**Green weekly checklist**

- [ ] R, P, D and E sessions done
- [ ] Daily-ish flashcard reviews
- [ ] Check-yourself questions answered from memory
- [ ] Deliverable committed
- [ ] [progress.md](../../roadmap/progress.md) updated and next week planned

## 🟡 Yellow: one heavy week

Triggered by a single significant exam or deadline that week, but not several colliding at once. The decision that matters here is which single practice session to keep: pick whichever is more time-sensitive (a machine you are mid-way through, or new material a later course depends on), and let the other wait a week rather than trying to compress both into less time than either needs.

| Keep | Drop or shrink |
|---|---|
| R session (can shrink to 45 minutes if genuinely needed) | Whichever of P or D you did not choose to keep |
| Daily flashcards, even just 5 minutes | E session, if the week truly has no room; resume it next week without guilt |
| The Sunday review, even in a shortened 15-minute form | The full deep-practice writeup; a short note of where you stopped is enough until next week |

**Yellow is not a punishment mode.** A Yellow week that still touched the material for 90 minutes and reviewed flashcards daily is a successful week by this system's own standard.

## 🔴 Red: crisis load

Triggered by finals week, several deadlines landing in the same days, or acute illness. The only goal in Red mode is **not losing the thread entirely**.

- **R session only**, and only if there is genuinely a spare 45 to 75 minutes; skip it without guilt if there is not.
- **Daily flashcards**, even reduced to 5 minutes, because this is the cheapest possible way to protect retention and the single highest-return habit in the whole system.
- Everything else (P, D, E, the weekly deliverable) explicitly **slides by a week** rather than being abandoned. Update [progress.md](../../roadmap/progress.md) to say so plainly, so the record is honest rather than silently incomplete.

A short Red week is easier to recover from than a full stop followed by the guilt of having "fallen behind," which is precisely why Red exists as a named, legitimate mode rather than an unplanned collapse.

## ⏸️ Pause: a genuine full stop

Some periods genuinely call for zero security work: a serious illness, a family emergency, an intentional, planned break. Pause differs from an unplanned lapse in one specific way: it comes with a **written resume plan**, decided either in advance or as soon as you are able to think clearly about it again.

A resume plan is short: the date or condition you expect to resume, and the single next action (reread last week's R session summary; redo the last deep-practice writeup's check-yourself questions) rather than trying to remember everything that was in flight. Writing this down, even briefly, is what turns a pause into a planned interruption instead of a quiet abandonment of the whole system.

## Break plan (intensive): 25 to 35 hours per week

Outside Green, Yellow, Red and Pause, university breaks are the one context where **more** time than the Green default is both available and worth using deliberately. Use one break per year as a genuine sprint: finishing a phase, running a specialization deep dive, or preparing for a certification exam. The pattern is five days on, two off, never seven.

| Day | Morning (3 h) | Afternoon (3 h) | Evening (1-2 h) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | New material for the phase or track | Guided labs on the day's topic | Notes and English listening |
| Tuesday | Theory and reading | Hands-on applying it | Writeup |
| Wednesday | Machine or project work | Continue; push through blockers | Notes |
| Thursday | New material | Labs | Speaking practice out loud |
| Friday | Project work | Finish and document | Writeup and publish |
| Saturday | Rest | Rest | Optional online CTF with a team |
| Sunday | Rest | Rest | Light review and plan the next week |

**Weekly goal during a break:** finish one phase block or one certification's worth of preparation, plus two published writeups or one portfolio project milestone. The two full rest days are not optional padding; the spacing and desirable-difficulty research in [Learning science](01-learning-science.md) both predict that an unbroken sprint with no recovery produces worse retention than the same hours with real rest built in.

## Mapping phases to the calendar

A guide, not a contract; move slower if a semester runs Yellow or Red more often than expected. The order of phases matters far more than hitting these specific dates.

| Period | Main focus | Typical mode |
|---|---|---|
| Semester 2 (now) | Phase 0, with Git and Linux first | Green, with Yellow during midterms and finals |
| Break after semester 2 | Finish Phase 0; start Phase 1 | Break plan |
| Semester 3 | Phase 1 (alongside Fundamentals of Cybersecurity), begin Phase 2 | Green, with Yellow during midterms and finals |
| Break | Phase 2 intensive: CTFs, wargames, first machines | Break plan |
| Semester 4 | Continue Phase 2; consolidate writeups | Green, with Yellow during midterms and finals |
| Semester 5 | Phase 3: run the six samplers | Green, with Yellow during midterms and finals |
| Break | Phase 3 deep dive in the chosen track | Break plan |
| Semester 6 | Phase 3 project; begin Phase 4 (first certification) | Green, with Yellow during midterms and finals |
| Semester 7 onward | Phase 4: certifications, projects, bug bounty, community | Green, adjusted around certification exam dates |

## Staying consistent across modes

- Put the Saturday deep-practice block in your calendar as a fixed appointment during every Green week.
- Track streaks in [progress.md](../../roadmap/progress.md); seeing a chain of completed weeks, including honestly logged Yellow and Red weeks, is motivating in itself.
- If you miss a week entirely with no mode logged at all, do not try to make it up in one marathon day; log it honestly and return to the normal plan.
- Review this page's mode assignments at the end of each semester against what actually happened, not what you had hoped, and adjust next semester's expectations accordingly.

## Practice

1. Look at your actual calendar for the next four weeks and assign each one a mode (Green, Yellow or Red) honestly, before the weeks happen, rather than deciding reactively.
2. Run one deliberate Yellow week exactly as specified (one practice session kept, daily flashcards, a shortened review) and confirm afterward that you protected the habit rather than losing the week entirely.
3. Write your own Pause resume plan template, so that if you ever need to use Pause mode for real, the decision of what to write is already made in advance.
4. At the end of your current semester, tally how many weeks were actually Green, Yellow, Red or Pause, and compare that against your assumption going in.

These support the [roadmap's phase structure](../../roadmap/README.md) and [the study system](02-the-study-system.md).
