# Community

Security is a small world, and most opportunities come through people. Being visible and helpful in these communities matters as much as any certificate. Dates and prices change every year, so confirm on the official site before you plan around an event.

## Colombia

### Conferences and events

| Event | Where | When | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| [DragonJAR Security Conference](https://www.dragonjar.org/) | Medellín | Usually September (12th edition in 2025) | The largest Spanish-language offensive security conference in Colombia; talks in Spanish |
| [BSides Colombia](https://sessionize.com/bsides-colombia-2026) | Rotates (Pereira in 2026, Bogotá in 2023) | Usually April | Community conference with talks, workshops, villages and a CTF; student-friendly |
| [ISACA Latin America / local chapter events](https://engage.isaca.org/) | Bogotá | Varies | Governance, risk and audit focus; the Bogotá chapter hosted the ISACA LatAm congress in 2025 |

Follow the organizers on social media for the next edition, since dates move year to year.

### At your university

The search for a cybersecurity student group at the Escuela Colombiana de Ingeniería Julio Garavito did not turn up a public one. That is an opportunity, not a dead end:

- Ask your Fundamentals of Cybersecurity professor whether a semillero (research seed group) exists or could be started.
- Start a small CTF team with classmates and register on [CTFtime](https://ctftime.org/). Three or four committed people is enough.
- Check the systems engineering department for research groups touching security, networks or cryptography.

A student-run CTF team or security club is one of the strongest things you can put on a CV and the easiest way to build the habit of practicing with others.

## Latin America

| Event | Country | When | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Ekoparty](https://www.ekoparty.org/) | Argentina (Buenos Aires) | Usually October (Oct 7-9 in 2026) | The longest-running and best-known hacker conference in Latin America; trainings, villages, CTF |
| [8.8 Computer Security Conference](https://www.8dot8.org/) | Chile (Santiago), and other editions | Usually October | Technical offensive security conference |
| [H2HC (Hackers to Hackers Conference)](https://www.h2hc.com.br/) | Brazil (São Paulo) | Usually late in the year | One of the oldest technical conferences in the region |

Latin America also fields a combined team, **CyberLATAM**, in the [International Cybersecurity Challenge](https://ecsc.eu/). Colombia has been among the participating countries. Strong CTF results can put you on the regional radar.

## Online communities

| Community | What it is | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| [TryHackMe](https://tryhackme.com/) and [Hack The Box](https://www.hackthebox.com/) Discord servers | Official community chat | Ask for nudges on rooms and machines, find teammates |
| [CTFtime](https://ctftime.org/) | CTF calendar and team rankings | Find online CTFs and a team to join |
| r/netsec, r/AskNetsec, r/cybersecurity | Reddit communities | News, career questions, discussion |
| Local and regional Discord/Telegram groups | Spanish-language chat | Many Colombian and LatAm practitioners organize here; ask at conferences for invites |
| Infosec on Mastodon and other networks | Practitioner posts | Researchers share findings and advisories; good for following the field |

A note on etiquette: ask specific questions, show what you have already tried, and never ask for or share flags or answers to active challenges. Helping others with method, not solutions, is how you build a reputation.
